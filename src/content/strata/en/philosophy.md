title: Philosophy
question: What is this, and how do we know?
method: conceptual analysis
description: The first stratum of the bridge. Clarifying what is being asked before answering it, by separating concepts, assumptions and criteria of truth.
---

Every piece of work begins before the model, the proof or the product: it begins with a question that does not yet
quite know what it is asking. This stratum takes care of that question. Here the vocabulary is examined, senses that
ordinary language runs together are told apart, and the assumptions that would otherwise decide the answer in silence
are made explicit.

The method is conceptual analysis. Asking “what is information?” requires separating the physical signal, the encoded
message and the meaning someone gives it; asking “how do we know?” requires saying what kind of evidence would count
for or against a claim. Neither question is settled by calculation, yet no later calculation makes sense without them.

In the symbology of the site, philosophy is the **point**, the 0-simplex: a position without extension. There is no
relation between terms yet, and no structure; there is only the place one starts from. Each following stratum adds one
dimension: the segment of theory, the triangle of formalism, the tetrahedron of value.

The texts in this stratum try to be honest about what they do not know. They state how confident each claim is, point
out where reasonable people disagree and cite the origin of every distinction, so that the reader can disagree
precisely.
