The last stratum asks about use. A correct result is not yet a good: it becomes useful when it solves someone’s
problem, and it can become harmful when its costs fall on people who did not choose to bear them. Here the work moves
from logical necessity to practical responsibility.

The method is design. To design is to decide under real constraints: time, money, energy, attention, accessibility.
Every decision favours some uses and hinders others and, for that reason, needs to be justified in public: what it is
for, whom it serves, at what cost and with which risks. Measuring only the benefit to those who build is a way of hiding
the cost to those who use.

In the symbology of the site, value is the **tetrahedron**, the 3-simplex: the first solid. It has volume, takes up
room in the world and can be touched. One of its edges is drawn dashed, because from any point of view something of the
artefact stays hidden; acknowledging that is part of the work.

The texts in this stratum deal with product, design and applied ethics. They try to make the criteria of evaluation
explicit, to record unintended effects when they appear, and to return to the earlier strata whenever a practical
consequence reveals a badly formulated concept. The bridge is crossed in one direction, but revised in both.
