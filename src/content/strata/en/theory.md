title: Theory
question: What model explains this?
method: modelling and abstraction
description: The second stratum. Building models that relate concepts, choosing what to abstract away and stating what is left out.
---

Once the question is clear, the next step is to relate. A theory links two or more concepts through a hypothesis that
can fail: entropy measures uncertainty; the sampling rate bounds the frequencies a signal can represent; small initial
differences grow in chaotic systems. A model is worth as much for what it explains as for what it allows us to predict
and, therefore, to test.

The method is modelling. To model is to choose what to ignore. Every model simplifies, and a simplification is
legitimate only when it is declared: which variables are in, which are out, at what scale and under which conditions the
explanation holds. A model that hides its own omissions is not simpler; it is only less honest.

In the symbology of the site, theory is the **segment**, the 1-simplex: two points and the relation between them. It
is the first structure with extension, but it is still open. A segment can be extended, moved or replaced by another
that joins the same endpoints better.

The texts in this stratum present models with their hypotheses in plain view, compare competing explanations when they
exist and point out what each would leave unexplained. The passage to the formal stratum happens when the relation can
be written so that its consequences follow without ambiguity.
