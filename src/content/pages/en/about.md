## The bridge

This site gathers essays, interactive labs and a map of concepts around one idea: knowledge can be walked like a bridge of four strata, always crossed in the same direction — from the question to the artefact.

| Stratum | Guiding question | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Philosophy | What is this, and how do we know? | conceptual analysis |
| Theory | Which model explains it? | modelling and abstraction |
| Formal | What follows necessarily? | proof, algorithm, determinism |
| Value | What is it for, for whom, at what cost? | design, product, ethics |

Each stratum is drawn as a simplex of increasing dimension — point, segment, triangle, tetrahedron — because each step adds one degree of determination, from the dimensionless question to the solid artefact. The order of the strata never rests on colour alone: shape, position, label and lightness repeat it, so that it survives every form of colour-vision deficiency, greyscale printing and screen readers.

The three opening essays make one complete crossing. [From sign to information](../essays/from-sign-to-information/) goes from philosophy to theory; [To sample is to decide](../essays/to-sample-is-to-decide/), from theory to the formal stratum; [Choosing costs bits](../essays/choosing-costs-bits/), from the formal stratum to value. The [labs](../lab/) make the theorems mentioned in the essays tangible, one at a time.

## How to read

There is no need to read in order. The essays are written to allow several paths:

- **Arguments with a shape.** The section headings of an essay state the role each part plays in the argument — question, definition, premise, claim, objection, reply, conclusion — and which other parts it depends on, adapting Toulmin's model of argument [@toulmin1958]. The argument can then be drawn as a graph and followed out of textual order.
- **Text that unfolds.** Sidenotes run alongside the text in the margin, and marked passages can be opened within the flow of reading, as in the stretchtext imagined by Ted Nelson, who also proposed the word “hypertext” [@nelson1965].
- **Figures that follow the scroll.** Some sequences advance as the page scrolls, and others cross the page sideways; without JavaScript, or with reduced motion, they remain readable step by step, without animation.
- **Linked concepts.** Technical terms lead to concept pages organised as a small ontology — in Gruber's sense, the specification of a representational vocabulary for a shared domain of discourse [@gruber1993]. Every item on the site is located by independent facets (stratum, form, domain, concepts), as in Ranganathan's faceted classification [@ranganathan1933], and the closeness between concepts is estimated with measures based on information content [@resnik1995; @lin1998; @seco2004].

## Epistemic status and provenance

Every essay and lab states how confident the author is and what kind of support its claims rest on. Confidence uses the scale of estimative expressions proposed by Sherman Kent, in which “probable” corresponds to about 75 %, give or take 12 points [@kent1964]; the kind of support is proof, empirical evidence, argument or speculation. Each text also reports its provenance: whether it was written without assistance, with assistance, or generated with AI help, and whether the author has reviewed it. The opening texts of this site were generated with AI assistance and are labelled as unreviewed until the author reviews them.

The essays exist in Portuguese and in English. Both versions were written to read naturally in each language rather than translated word for word, so they may differ in phrasing. Citations follow the Brazilian ABNT standards in Portuguese and APA style in English; the [colophon](../colophon/) has the details.
